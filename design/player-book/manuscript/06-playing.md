# Playing the Game

## The core roll

When an action is uncertain and the consequence matters, roll **3d6 + one attribute**.

| Total | Result |
|---:|---|
| **8 or less** | **Weak** |
| **9–14** | **Standard** |
| **15 or more** | **Strong** |

No roll is needed when the action is routine, impossible, or has no meaningful cost. The fiction resolves those cases. A Weak result changes the degree, cost or consequence of the action; it does not automatically mean the character did nothing.

## Boons and Banes

A **Boon** adds a die; keep the highest three. A **Bane** adds a die; keep the lowest three. They cancel one for one. After cancelling, no more than three net extra dice are rolled.

If the retained dice show three natural **1s**, add a complication to a Weak result. Three natural **6s** add a benefit to a Strong result. Read the retained dice before adding the attribute.

## Your turn

A character has:

- one **Main Action**;
- one **Maneuver**;
- movement up to **Speed**;
- one **Reaction** on its stated trigger.

Characters alternate turns by side. The order within a side is chosen as play proceeds. An ability states which part of the budget it uses. A defense roll does not by itself spend a Reaction.

## Movement and range

The table uses **squares**. One square is roughly two metres. **Adjacent** means within one square with an accessible path. A visible target still needs a clear attack path.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Adjacent** | Within 1 square, with a path |
| **Close** | Within 4 squares |
| **Far** | Within 12 squares |
| **Extreme** | Beyond 12 squares; usually requires a vehicle or ship weapon |

Difficult terrain costs 2 squares of movement for 1 square entered. Standing from Prone costs half your movement. You can move through an ally's square but not end there; you cannot move through an enemy's square.

## Attacks and defence

A player attack is one roll against the target's defence. The SA sets the defence from the target's attributes and situation, or uses the target's printed defence. A player area attack uses one roll with the worst applicable Boon/Bane situation among the targets; each target is affected by the result.

When an NPC attacks, each affected player makes a defence roll if the ability calls for one. The NPC's printed attribute is the attacker's approach, not the attribute the player rolls.

## Damage and conditions

Damage has a **type**: Kinetic, Thermal, Cryogenic, Electrical, Corrosive, Sonic or Radiation. Resistance applies to each component separately. A condition is not a damage type: Burning, Corroded, Frozen, Stunned, Dazed, Deafened, Disrupted, Prone, Slowed and Bleeding are conditions.

A condition with a rating (for example, **Bleeding 2**) stacks its rating where the condition says so. A binary condition such as **Prone** does not become Prone 2. A condition's own rules set its duration; an attack with an Instant duration does not make the condition last forever.

## Social play

Social abilities create leverage, information or progress. They do not take control of another character. The SA sets what a reasonable person would concede and what would require a real cost. A single roll does not replace a conversation when the players' choices and the NPC's interests matter.

## Exploration and technical play

Exploration uses the same roll and the same three bands. A scan, a survey, a repair or a route-plot is a progress task: the SA sets the progress needed (usually 3), and each result adds 1, 2 or 3. Progress persists unless the situation changes.

## Ship stations

When the crew is aboard a ship, a character may take a **station**: Pilot, Navigator, Engineer, Gunner, Sensor Operator or Commander. A station is a job in the scene, not a class. Anyone qualified can take it. Class abilities make a character especially good at a station without making it exclusive.

## The SA's job

The Sector Architect describes the galaxy, portrays its people, presents danger and adjudicates uncertainty. The SA should say **what is at stake** before calling for a roll, and should let a good plan work without dice when the outcome is not in doubt.
