# Disciplines

Disciplines represent training in fields such as Engineering and Piloting. Buy a rank to meet the requirements of abilities in that field. When you use one of those abilities, roll its listed attribute.

## Ranks and prices

Disciplines have ranks **1, 2 and 3**. Buy them in order: rank 2 requires rank 1, and rank 3 requires rank 2. A class sets the price of each rank.

| Price band | Rank 1 | Rank 2 | Rank 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Home** | 1 DP | 2 DP | 4 DP |
| **Adjacent** | 2 DP | 4 DP | 8 DP |
| **Foreign** | 3 DP | 6 DP | 12 DP |
| **Opposed** | 4 DP | 8 DP | 16 DP |

Pay for each rank separately at the price listed for your class. A Technician buying Engineering 1 and 2 pays 1 DP for the first rank and 2 DP for the second.

## Choose what you want to do

Start with a situation you expect to face. If you want to keep a ship flying after a hit, look for an Engineering ability such as Keep It Running. If you would rather see the hit coming, look at the options for sensors, navigation and piloting. Check the card's rank requirements, then find the cost for your class in the tables that follow.

The fields often meet at the same problem. An Engineer repairs a drone's damaged housing. A Robotics specialist changes how the drone responds to a command. A Computing specialist works on access to the network it uses. Look at the abilities you want to learn to see where each route leads.

You can attempt an ordinary action without buying a discipline rank. A character without Gunnery can still fire a weapon. A rank gives access to cards that offer more ways to use it. A narrow specialist can spend deeply in one field, and a broad character can learn rank-1 options for several kinds of work. Each ability lists its own prerequisites.
