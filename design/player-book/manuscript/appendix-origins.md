# Star Systems and Species

These ten star systems give crews places to start and characters places to call home. Each entry describes a lineage associated with the system, a place to arrive and a problem a crew can take on. The associations are historical. People move between systems, and ports serve many species.

**Species baseline.** These are descriptions for character creation, illustration and scenes. Size and lifespan are broad adult reference ranges for this review. All ten breathe ordinary habitable air, use the shared personal harm and recovery rules, and can handle standard crew controls and gear fitted to their bodies. Their movement and senses follow the common rules. Use the standard action and equipment limits regardless of limb count. See Chapter 3 for selectable traits and Chapter 2 for starting values.

## Junction · Humans

**System.** A single yellow star lights the settlements of Junction. Waycross is a station cluster built where older transit rings meet. Its public concourse connects pressurized docks, a surface shuttle terminal and a repair market. Waycross posts ferry notices in Galactic Standard and Junction Portspeech.

**Species.** Humans are upright bipeds with two grasping hands and varied skin, hair and builds. Adults commonly stand 1.45–2.05 m and live 70–110 years. Waycross is a longstanding diaspora center. Records of the earliest human settlements survive in several systems.

**Arrival.** A passenger ring has lost pressure in one corridor. Its two surviving docks use different fittings, and the crew needs to move evacuees between them.

## Kett · Tessari

**System.** An orange star warms Kett Anchor, a dense rocky planet cut by ravines. Settlements straddle the sheltered walls, with freight suspended from gantries. Crews transfer at Counterspan Orbital and descend in licensed cargo lifts. Counterspan is common on the lifts and in Kett Anchor’s freight towns.

**Species.** Tessari stand on three short, jointed legs. Two grasping arms hang below an arch of segmented collarbones that shelters the head. Dense bones and flexible cartilage spread a load across the body, a useful build on Kett Anchor’s steep, heavy ground. Adults commonly stand 1.40–1.85 m and live 80–125 years.

**Arrival.** A shifting freight cradle has trapped a work crew above a ravine. The counterweight is accessible from the adjacent maintenance cage.

## Arlet · Orivane

**System.** A red star lights a gas giant whose rubble moons support the Lattice Moons stations. The Orivane lineage developed across rotating habitats there. Docking crews meet arrivals at an outward-facing transfer hub before assigning a route to a smaller station. Transfer crews use Lattice Sign across windows and noisy loading decks.

**Species.** Orivane have elongated forearms, hinged legs and wide grasping feet. Two balance sacs sit behind a flattened jaw. Their long reach and multiple points of purchase suit a habitat where work often takes place between handholds. Adults commonly stand 1.65–2.15 m and live 75–115 years.

**Arrival.** A compartment is rotating off its normal axis with a casualty inside. A tether team needs a ship to hold position while they make the transfer.

## Brack · Nacreth

**System.** Brack is a single-star system with a tidally active ocean planet, Siltwake. Dry settlements connect to submerged caverns through lock chambers. The port above High Shoal routes cargo through a surface float and a pressure dock. Pressure-dock instructions are posted in Standard and Shoal Speech.

**Species.** A low mantle and several muscular support lobes give Nacreth a broad stance on shifting tidal ground. Two dexterous palps handle equipment. Pressure-sensitive cheek folds flank a ribbed air siphon. Adults commonly stand 1.45–1.95 m and live 75–120 years. They use fitted breathing equipment underwater.

**Arrival.** A failed intake pump has cut cooling to a pressure dock. The port can lend diving gear if the crew can find the damaged fitting before the next tide.

## Khor · Silev

**System.** Knell Basin, the inhabited inner planet, orbits a small orange star. Heating and cooling set its long rock seams vibrating. The basin's quarry port connects its settlements by freight rail and maintains a quiet test hall beneath the landing apron. Knell Pattern travels between stations on compatible contact pads.

**Species.** Silev move on four stout legs and work with two forward arms. Flexible mineral plates cover the ribs and forearms. A low, wide body stays close to the trembling shelves of Knell Basin. Adults commonly stand 1.50–2.00 m and live 90–145 years. Their traits describe how contact with a vibrating surface can aid a check.

**Arrival.** A damaged power conduit rings through several similar support frames. A shutdown would strand a train in the basin.

## Vey · Ambril

**System.** Morrowline lies along the day-night boundary of a planet that keeps one face toward its red star. Shelter chains run across the boundary, trading power from the lit side for cold storage on the dark side. Arrivals land at the terminator exchange. Shelter route books use Morrowline and distinguish warm and cold approaches.

**Species.** Three splayed legs carry an Ambril’s radial body. Two working arms reach beneath a pleated heat-exchange collar surrounding the sensory crown. The collar exposes more or less surface as conditions change along Morrowline’s warm and cold routes. Adults commonly stand 1.45–1.95 m and live 80–125 years. Ambril follow the standard rules for thermal protection.

**Arrival.** A shelter's warm-side corridor is blocked. A medic and a patient need transport across a cold service deck before the next shift.

## Osmere · Pellori

**System.** Reedglass is a wetland world orbiting a pale yellow star. Its lowland cities stand above shifting water and maintain independent grow beds. A quarantine quay receives foreign plants and living cargo. Quarantine staff use Reedglass Speech in local records and Standard at the foreign quay.

**Species.** A Pellori grows as an interwoven mesh around one nervous system. The upright body branches above flexible supports and two jointed handling tendrils. Exposed sensory patches vary in texture around some living cultures, a useful way to sample Reedglass’s crowded wetlands. Adults commonly stand 1.35–1.85 m and live 85–140 years. Pellori use fitted protective gear under quarantine procedures.

**Arrival.** Three grow beds have developed the same discoloration. Their shared water supply passed inspection, but the newest patch is spreading toward the port.

## Neral · Lethren

**System.** Gloam Arc is a moon that spends long intervals in its parent planet's shadow. Its archive settlements use shaded shafts with retractable surface panels. Offworld crews land at Lantern Gate on the rim of the main shaft. Custodians use Lantern Sign when light permits and keep signed records on video.

**Species.** Lethren have folded walking limbs, narrow bodies and a large ocular lens beneath paired, patterned cheek sails. Their eyes are suited to making out shapes in Gloam Arc’s long periods of dim light. Adults commonly stand 1.45–1.95 m and live 70–115 years. They rely on available light or equipment to see. Agreed signals depend on what the observer has learned.

**Arrival.** Smoke has forced an archive evacuation. Its custodians need a recorder brought out before the entrance shutters cycle.

## Tal Oren · Inkleaf

**System.** Tern Orchard circles a temperate world in a system with a single white star. Suspended gardens grew around the stations where the first uplifted canopy browsers settled. A cargo dock at the garden ring accepts crews for maintenance and survey work. Garden crews use Orchard Register to distinguish the levels of the trellis.

**Species.** Inkleaf descend from segmented canopy browsers. Four climbing limbs distribute their weight across thin branches and two forward limbs handle tools. Their flexible wrists and broad pads also suit the delicate trellises of Tern Orchard. Adults commonly stand 1.20–1.65 m and live 60–100 years. Grip and balance depend on usable supports.

**Arrival.** A cable has caught on a live trellis over a sealed cultivation bay. The bay operators need it back without losing the plants below.

## Ferric · Weldkin

**System.** Kiln Nine is a former foundry habitat in the belt of a single orange star. Descendants of its grown industrial organisms now live in several settlements. Kiln Nine's active docks trade in ceramic parts, tools and reclaimable scrap. Workshops mark reusable ceramic tags in Kiln Speech.

**Species.** Weldkin are angular, grown organisms with dark living skin, pale woven tendons and forked leg joints. The original foundry bodies were designed for precise control under industrial loads. Their descendants have ordinary biological care needs under the shared rules. Adults commonly stand 1.55–2.10 m and live 80–130 years.

**Arrival.** A recovery harness keeps failing during a salvage lift. Its owner suspects the new anchor, while the rig crew points to a hairline defect in the load frame.
