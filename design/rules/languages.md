# Languages and translation

**Status:** Playtest proposal. Names, communities and translator prices are working material.

## Choose your languages

Begin with **Galactic Standard** and **one other ordinary language**. Galactic Standard is the shared language of trade and travel. The SA can name a local equivalent before character creation. Choose your second language from the ordinary catalog or name an ordinary language with the SA. Think about who taught it to you. A neighbor, a work crew or a foster family can matter as much as the system where you were born.

Record the language by name on your sheet. This choice costs no DP and is separate from your culture and background grants. Any ancestry can learn any ordinary language. The system associations describe where a language is used. They give no automatic fluency, and no language is a requirement for playing an ancestry.

## What fluency covers

Knowing a language lets you produce and understand its ordinary forms and read and write its usual script if it has one. A written-only, spoken-only or comprehension-only grant keeps that restriction. Record it beside the name. An ordinary-language grant can include a signed language. A spoken-only grant cannot. You need a usable channel to communicate. Sign requires visibility and usable gestures. Speech requires a medium or a working link. A fitted pad or terminal can reproduce a patterned signal where the equipment supports it. Knowing how to communicate grants no extra sense or limb.

If you know the language and can perceive the message, understand its ordinary meaning without rolling. An argument, a deceptive statement or a damaged record can still call for an attribute check. Choose the attribute for the actual task, such as Reason to interpret evidence or Guile to recognize a deception, and use one roll. Knowing a language gives no authority, clearance or truth detection.

## Ordinary languages of the ten systems

| Language | System and ancestry association | Where you encounter it |
|---|---|---|
| Junction Portspeech | Junction · Humans | Waycross neighborhoods use this spoken language at home and in the repair markets. Ferry notices often appear in both Portspeech and Standard. |
| Counterspan | Kett · Tessari | A spoken language with a compact written script used on Kett Anchor and the orbital freight lifts. Weight, height and direction are explicit in many everyday phrases. |
| Lattice Sign | Arlet · Orivane | A full signed language used across the Lattice Moons. Recordings preserve motion and orientation. Printed diagrams can convey familiar signs but are not a separate script. |
| Shoal Speech | Brack · Nacreth | A spoken language with a written form used around Siltwake’s tidal settlements. Dry-lock instructions often specify the stage of the tide before the place. |
| Knell Pattern | Khor · Silev | A language of short patterned pulses and a corresponding written notation. It is used in homes and along Knell Basin’s freight rail. Working contact pads can transmit it across a compatible line. |
| Morrowline | Vey · Ambril | A spoken language used at the terminator shelters and in their route books. Speakers often locate a destination by naming its warm or cold approach. |
| Reedglass Speech | Osmere · Pellori | A spoken language with a script used in family records, grow beds and quarantine stations. Local instructions often distinguish individual samples from whole beds. |
| Lantern Sign | Neral · Lethren | A signed language used in Gloam Arc’s archives and homes. It uses silhouette, placement and motion and needs enough light to see. Archive recordings can preserve the signs. |
| Orchard Register | Tal Oren · Inkleaf | A spoken language with a script used in Tern Orchard’s schools, gardens and repair crews. Garden directions often name the level before the route. |
| Kiln Speech | Ferric · Weldkin | A spoken language with a written form used on Kiln Nine. Foundry workers also print short instructions on reusable ceramic tags. |

Lattice Sign, Lantern Sign and Hand Cant are separate languages. Recognizing a gesture in one does not teach the others. Knell Pattern can be communicated through the proper equipment or read from notation without a character having a special vibration sense.

## Languages across systems

| Language | Where you encounter it |
|---|---|
| Galactic Standard | Inter-system traffic, docking offices, mixed crews and many public services. |
| Station Cant | Free stations and salvage communities. A port may use its own slang within the language. |
| Hand Cant | A full signed language shared by crews that work in vacuum or around loud equipment. |
| Corporate Registry | A spoken and written language used in some corporate arcologies and charter offices. Fluency grants no legal expertise. |
| Frontier Relay | A trade language used among scattered settlements along irregular freight routes. |
| Named local language | Choose one with the SA. Record its actual name and where you learned it. |

Some collections preserve **Old Terra** or a local **Threshold Liturgy**. These are uncommon, named options that require explicit campaign permission or a grant that allows them. Threshold Liturgy refers to one tradition chosen by the SA. It grants no knowledge of every ancient inscription or control system.

## Translation and first contact

A handheld translator works when it has a library for the language and can capture its channel. It can convey a routine request, a warning or the literal wording of a recorded message. Check an ambiguous term with a speaker or a second example. Translation does not determine intent or grant clearance. A ship terminal can use the same procedure if the crew has a working interface and relevant library. This creates no ship module or sensor ability.

When the crew has no shared language, find a channel both parties can use. Agree on one concrete idea to exchange, such as stop, safe passage or the identity of an object. Show or demonstrate it and invite the other party to repeat or point back. When the result is uncertain and the parties are willing, roll Reason once. Weak establishes what the other party noticed or where the mismatch lies. Standard confirms the one intended signal. Strong also confirms one contrasting signal, such as go alongside stop. For sustained work, set a progress target and use a card such as Establish Protocol or First Contact. A shared vocabulary from those cards covers only its agreed terms and ends as printed.

A cipher, access key or signed credential is a technical obstacle. Fluency lets you interpret readable text. It grants no decryption or authentication. Where a roll is needed, use Knowledge or the explicit attribute of an applicable card. Set the technical goal and costs before rolling. Solving a cipher exposes its contents in the original language.

## Learn in play

Record a new language when a trait, ability or agreed campaign grant provides it. A teacher, regular practice or a useful archive can explain how a character learns. Agree on the time, access and scope before committing. There is no universal DP purchase price. Using a translator or completing a first-contact task does not grant fluency.
