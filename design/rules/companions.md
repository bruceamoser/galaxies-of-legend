# Drones, sprites and companions

**Status:** Playtest proposal. These are personal-scale profiles for a crew that needs an extra tool or specialist. Choose a profile from the companion appendix of the Sector Architect's Book. Its printed access, actions and limits apply even when a similar adversary has more capabilities.

## Acquire and assign

A **drone** is equipment. Buy or borrow the listed chassis and pair it with a compatible controller before the scene. A **sprite** is a licensed software agent. Load it onto a working data slate or accessible terminal before the scene. A **hired companion** is a consenting NPC. Agree on their job, fee, risks and exit terms before they join. The fees are sample market quotes in coins, subject to availability.

A profile lists its minimum controller level. This is a playtest access gate, separate from the price or the crew's reputation. It adds no discipline bonus and costs no DP. Ownership does not bypass the gate. Name one eligible controller for each profile before entering danger. A controller can direct one companion at a time. Change the assignment when both controllers and the companion can communicate and are out of immediate danger. No companion can direct another companion.

## Spend an action to direct

Directing takes no roll. On your turn, spend one of these actions:

| You spend | The companion receives |
|---|---|
| Main Action | One Main Action and one Maneuver. It can convert either action to movement through the ordinary conversions. |
| Maneuver | One Maneuver and its ordinary movement. It may convert the Maneuver normally. |

Resolve the package immediately on your turn. A companion receives one package per round, even if another card or a change of controller could otherwise direct it again. It can use its printed actions, Defend, and ordinary movement or interaction consistent with its body, tools and permissions. Defend gives it one Boon on defense rolls until the start of your next turn. It can handle objects only as its profile allows. Pay any printed cost in addition to the package. A card that already directs a robot, such as Remote Hands, uses that robot's package for the round and follows the card's own procedure.

A companion gets no separate turn, Reaction, opportunity attack, unattended scouting, ongoing watch, autonomous alert or combat activity from a standing order. For a task with a printed time, remain present and actively direct it throughout. A prior order never pays for another round. Read condition ticks and references to the companion's turn on the controller's turn, even if the companion receives no package. Resolve attacks and defenses with the companion's printed attributes and the normal single-roll procedure. Apply the controller's personal abilities only if they explicitly direct or affect the companion.

## Reach, interruption and damage

A drone needs a working controller and a clear command signal within **8 squares**, measured between nearest occupied squares. A hired companion needs a way to understand the order within **8 squares**, whether by voice, agreed signal or working comms. Blocking the link or incapacitating the controller ends an unfinished order. A drone stops where it is. A hired person can leave danger or withdraw from the job as an NPC under the SA's control. This grants no additional crew-controlled action, and a hire can refuse orders outside their agreed job.

A sprite works only on its current host and on services that host is already authorized to access. It occupies no square, carries nothing, and cannot be attacked directly with a personal weapon. It needs a working operator interface within reach. Unplugging the host, losing access, or incapacitating the controller stops the active task. It resumes only with a new paid order after access is restored. Sprites cannot create credentials, operate another companion, or perform a physical task through a connected device.

Drones and hired companions occupy one square and can be targeted, moved or impeded under the ordinary rules. At 0 HP a drone is disabled and stays in place. It receives no ordinary HP healing. A workshop can restore it to full HP in one hour with suitable tools and parts costing one quarter of its listed price, rounded up, if the chassis can be recovered and repaired. A hired companion is an ordinary living NPC for healing and defeat. At 0 HP they are removed from the fight and the SA determines their fate from the situation. They have no player Grit, death marks or Last Words. Sprites have no HP and become unavailable when their host or license is lost.

These procedures cover personal companions. The Starship Manual governs ship stations, system dice, vehicle-scale actions and ship repair.

## At the table

Iona spends her Maneuver to direct a Pinhole Scout. It moves up to four squares, then uses Peer Through at a vent opening. She can see what the camera reports during that order. If she uses her Main Action to repair the vent instead of directing the scout next round, the scout stays where it is and sends no new report.

A controller spends a Main Action to direct a Guard Tripod. The tripod converts its Maneuver into movement, gets a clear line, then fires. The player rolls the tripod's Agility and reads its printed outcomes. If an opponent later passes next to the tripod, it makes no opportunity attack.
