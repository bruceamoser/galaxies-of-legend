# Environmental hazards

**Status:** Untested GoL playtest proposal. Resolve damage, conditions, Grit, Dying and wounds under the normal personal harm rules.

## Enter an unsafe area

Before the crew commits, state the observable hazard, who is exposed, the trigger or interval, the consequence, and what interrupts it. Track only intervals that actually elapse. If the crew leaves before an interval ends, retain its elapsed exposure until the scene ends or the hazard is made safe. Apply a contact hazard when contact happens. Apply a continuing hazard only on its declared interval, once per exposed character. Establish separate triggers before treating different sources as separate events.

## Move without normal gravity

In low gravity, use ordinary Speed where there is reliable footing. The SA marks routes where a leap or loose load will carry farther than expected before the crew commits. In high gravity, an unacclimated explorer takes 1 Bane on sustained Brawn or Fortitude effort and loses 1 Speed, minimum 0, while exposed. A character is acclimated only if the fiction or an explicit trait establishes it.

In zero gravity, move normally along reachable fixed handholds with a free hand, or along a suitable surface with engaged mag boots. A tether limits how far you can drift. Pulling yourself along a taut line without fixed handholds costs 2 movement per square and requires a free hand. A trait such as Tether Runner can change this cost. Unbraced explorers take 1 Bane on Agility checks and melee attack rolls in zero gravity. A stable hold, engaged mag boots, a taut anchor line, or a trait that explicitly removes the Bane provides the applicable exception.

To cross open space, spend a Maneuver to push off a reachable anchor or use a working thruster. Pick a straight direction and distance up to Speed. Move that distance now. If still unbraced at the start of your next turn, drift that distance in the same direction before acting. Record direction and distance. Catch a reachable stable hold with a free hand to stop during that movement. A working thruster can stop or redirect drift with a Maneuver during your turn. Ordinary grid movement does not change drift without something to push against.

When drift meets a solid obstacle or a creature that cannot clear the path, use the existing collision procedure. Use the remaining movement for collision damage. For a push or pull in zero gravity, use the printed movement and ordinary Stability. A forced push that ends in open space starts drift in its direction at the distance actually moved. A later push replaces the recorded vector. Forced movement never provokes.

## Keep track of breathable air

A working sealed vac suit prevents direct pressure exposure and supplies six hours of air. Record elapsed use in minutes. One minute of exertion uses six minutes of its air budget. For short scenes, total time at the end of the scene and charge at least one minute of elapsed use if air was flowing. Ordinary use is one minute per minute. Turn off or refill the suit only where the wearer has a safe air supply or a compatible isolated changeover connection. A compatible refill port takes ten minutes. A spare cylinder replenishes the supply printed for the device it fits. A rebreather has one hour of air at its stated use and provides no pressure protection.

Without pressure protection in vacuum, take 1 Physical damage at the end of each exposed turn. Without breathable air, count consecutive turns ending without a breath. The first three such turns add no suffocation damage. On the fourth and each later turn take 3 Physical damage, bypassing resistance, for lack of air. These are separate sources with their own triggers. Reaching breathable air ends the suffocation count immediately. A sealed suit with exhausted air prevents the pressure event but does not stop the suffocation count. If exposure reduces HP to zero, use the ordinary Dying procedure.

Outside combat, use turn order while anyone is in immediate danger from pressure loss or lack of air. A suit seal, restored atmosphere or rescue stops only the exposure it actually addresses. A repaired breach does not restore air that has already escaped.

## Heat, cold and radiation

For prolonged extreme heat or cold, start a three-segment exposure clock. Mark one segment per ten minutes exposed. At three, apply Burning 1 for heat or Freezing 1 for cold, clear the clock, and start it again if exposure continues. During a dangerous scene that has no combat turn order, resolve the condition's first normal end-of-turn damage and decay when it is applied. Apply ordinary resistance, condition decay and wound rules. Active flame or a cryogenic spill uses its own immediate trigger instead of charging both procedures for the same contact. A suitable shelter or rated equipment prevents exposure it actually blocks. A safe ten-minute interval clears this clock.

A radiation source specifies its affected area, interval and Radiant damage before exposure. A marked contaminated compartment can use 2 Radiant damage per ten minutes as an untested fixture. Apply Radiant resistance and ordinary harm, including wounds only when the usual rules call for one. Personal armor protects only against the types it names. Use a meter to find a change in local radiation, then leave, shield or isolate the source.
