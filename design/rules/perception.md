# Perception and contacts

**Status:** Playtest proposal. The single-roll search and hiding procedure follows HoL. The sight and sensor details are GoL test fixtures.

## Establish what is perceptible

Describe obvious sights, sounds and instrument readings without a roll. Establish the observer's position, line of sight and available senses. An opaque obstruction or total darkness blocks ordinary sight. Sound travels through air or a connected material, as appropriate. Ordinary speech cannot cross vacuum. Darkness and smoke do not apply the Blinded condition.

| Visibility | Sight-based rolls |
|---|---|
| Clear | No modifier |
| Dim light or light haze | 1 Bane |
| Very dim light or heavy haze | 2 Banes |
| Barely perceptible light or near-opaque haze | 3 Banes |
| Total darkness or opaque obstruction | Sight unavailable |

Use the worse of illumination and obscurement after applying any ability that reduces one source. A Lethren with Twilight Eyes reduces dim-light Banes by one. It does not reduce smoke. Modifiers from other sources use the ordinary Boon and Bane rules. If the task needs sight and sight is unavailable, change the approach or restore visibility before rolling.

## Search and hide

Searching or attempting to hide in combat takes a Main Action unless a card says otherwise. A player actively searching for an uncertain clue, creature or object rolls Reason. Before the roll, name the area and what can be found. Weak narrows the search or reveals a clue at a cost agreed before rolling. Standard locates the target if it can be perceived. Strong also reveals one observable detail about its position, route or recent activity.

A player hiding rolls Agility when their position can conceal them. Weak reaches concealment if the route is clear, but leaves their position known. Standard makes them Hidden. Strong also lets them conceal one trace their movement would otherwise leave if they can reach it during the action. No roll hides someone in plain view. When an NPC would plausibly discover a Hidden player, the player rolls Agility to remain Hidden. On Weak the NPC discovers them. On Standard or Strong they remain Hidden if their concealment still works. A player searching for an NPC rolls Reason. Resolve an incident with one roll and do not add a second opposed roll.

Hidden is universal. Attacking or openly revealing yourself ends it. Noise, displaced objects, footprints and sensors can reveal a location if they are capable of detecting the evidence. Knowing a square is occupied does not give sight or make a sight-required attack eligible. If a sense positively reveals the creature, Hidden ends. A vague contact alone does not end it.

## Read a sensor contact

Declare the sensor, its usable range and what it can measure. A personal sensor pack covers its current room or one nearby outdoor area within 20 squares if the signal reaches it. Solid shielding can block a scan. A ship sensor uses the Starship Manual's effective system die, range bands and operating procedure.

| Information | What the crew can act on |
|---|---|
| No contact | The instrument has no return. A blank display cannot distinguish empty space from blocked coverage. |
| Contact | Bearing or approximate location and one measurable return, such as heat or movement. |
| Classified | A supported category or signature, such as a running motor or several moving bodies. |
| Identified | A match to known reference data or a particular measurable feature, such as the location of an active transmitter. |

Use one Reason check for an uncertain active scan unless the station or ability names a different procedure. A routine known reading needs no roll. A substantial search can use a progress track, with goal and consequences announced before rolling. Sensor Sweep supplies its own outcomes. A working jam can block coverage or impose a declared Bane where a return still gets through. Describe which before the roll. Mark when a result was recorded. A stale contact gives a last known location and needs a fresh reading to confirm movement.

A sensor supplies only evidence its hardware can measure. It grants neither visual targeting through a barrier nor a weapon lock. For ship combat, use the Starship Manual's targeting, system check and range rules.
