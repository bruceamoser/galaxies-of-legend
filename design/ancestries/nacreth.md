# Nacreth

**Status:** Playtest

**Starting values:** HP +2, base Speed 6, +0 coins.

**Origin:** Siltwake

Nacreth communities built the tidal locks of Siltwake. Their engineers and brokers are used to working with neighbors who need the same water for entirely different purposes.

## Appearance

A low mantle and muscular support lobes spread a Nacreth’s weight over shifting ground. Two palps manipulate tools. Folds around a ribbed air siphon register changes in water flow. Nacreth breathe air and use fitted equipment underwater.

## Home and history

On Siltwake in Brack, cargo once waited for the tide to reach the mouths of natural caverns. Settlements built gates to extend their loading hours. When one of the early gates trapped water upstream, its operators discovered that a good day at their dock could flood a neighbor’s storehouse.

## Society and relations

Lock schedules are now negotiated across several settlements. Many dock councils set a public time for objections before changing a gate cycle. Inland families often think the councils have too much authority, while port operators point out who has to answer when a schedule fails. Visitors who come with a clear timetable usually get further than those who arrive with a title.

## Before the gates open

At several Siltwake docks, operators lay out tomorrow’s gate schedule where anyone waiting for a shipment can see it. A person asking for a change names the settlement that will take the extra water. When the notice board moves indoors, people know to ask who is being kept out of the meeting.

## Explorers

Service Cooperative riggers learn to keep a pressure joint serviceable until the next tide. Contact Port cargo factors broker passages when schedules conflict. A Technician may leave to learn how other worlds handle water. An Envoy may be carrying a dispute that two councils could never settle at home.

**Your explorer.** What cargo did you once persuade a dock to hold? Who paid for the delay?

## Rules

Choose two traits. Each trait states its benefit, action and limits. Apply the normal Boon and Bane rules and equipment requirements.

1. **Read the Water.** *The pull on your mantle tells you where the water is going.* While in contact with moving water, you know the direction of the local current. Gain 1 Boon on checks to locate its visible or reachable source.
2. **Tidal Walker.** *Your support lobes spread over a surface that would shift under a boot.* Shallow water and ordinary mud do not increase your voluntary movement cost. Deep water and other hazards use their normal rules.
3. **Under the Surface.** *You save your strength for the work below.* Swimming in calm water costs you 1 movement per square. Use normal breathing equipment and roll for consequential swimming hazards.
4. **Hold Against the Surge.** *You settle your weight against the pull.* Once per scene, when a current or surge would forcibly move you and you have a fixed support within reach, spend your Reaction to reduce that movement by up to 2 squares.
5. **Seal Keeper.** *You have seen what a hairline leak can do.* Gain 1 Boon on checks to locate or repair a leak in a suit, hatch or pressure line you can access. Use the usual tools and protection.
