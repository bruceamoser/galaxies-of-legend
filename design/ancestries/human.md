# Human

**Status:** Playtest

**Starting values:** HP +2, base Speed 6, +0 coins.

**Origin:** Waycross

Human settlements are scattered across the frontier. Waycross, a meeting point for families who arrived on different ships and never agreed on a single way to live, is one of their largest homes.

## Appearance

Humans vary widely in height, complexion and build. Most shipyards stock Human-sized fittings because Human crews helped establish early freight routes. A Human raised on a low-gravity ring can still find a planet’s weight exhausting.

## Home and history

Waycross grew around the transfer rings in Junction. The first permanent residents were passengers stranded when a carrier withdrew service. They converted waiting rooms into kitchens and paid for repairs by taking cargo from ships whose crews could not agree on fittings or schedules.

## Society and relations

The rings elect their own berth councils. A common custom is to offer a new arrival a place at dinner before asking whom they know. Established families can be generous at their tables and fiercely protective of their docking rights. The older rings call that stewardship. Recent arrivals call it a closed shop.

## A spare seat

At the end of a Waycross shift, some households set out one more bowl than they need. A visitor can eat first and say what brought them to the ring afterward. On the oldest rings, a household may invite the same guest back a second night to hear the part of the story they left out.

## Explorers

Contact Port interpreters learn how to settle arguments between crews who disagree about what was promised. Trade Diaspora families keep routes alive through relatives and old debts. An Envoy might leave to win a berth for a neighborhood that has outgrown its ring. A Spacer might leave because the council expects them to inherit a job they never wanted.

**Your explorer.** Who gave you a seat when you first arrived somewhere alone? Is there anyone you would refuse the same courtesy now?

**At the table.** A Human with Make It Fit turns a difficult lift into a two-person job. When their partner is about to roll to maneuver a crate, they spend their Reaction and explain how they guide it from the other side.

## Rules

Choose two traits. Each trait states its benefit, action and limits. Apply the normal Boon and Bane rules and equipment requirements.

1. **Make It Fit.** *You find a job for the person standing beside you.* Once per scene, when an adjacent ally is about to roll for a noncombat task you can physically help with, spend your Reaction to give that roll 1 Boon.
2. **Learn on the Job.** *You make a little time to learn what everyone else takes for granted.* Gain 1 extra DP in your level-0 purchase pool and 1 extra DP when you reach level 3. Each grant follows the rules for that pool.
3. **Second Approach.** *You take the hint and change the plan.* Once per scene, after a Weak result on a noncombat progress task, gain 1 Boon on your next roll on that task before the scene ends if you use a different attribute and explain the new approach.
4. **Get There First.** *When the call comes, you are already moving.* At the start of your first turn in combat, gain 2 extra squares of movement for that turn.
5. **A Name at Every Dock.** *Someone in this port knows someone you helped.* Once per visit to a populated port, spend ten minutes asking around. The SA names a willing local who can introduce you to a relevant person or business, if one is present. The contact sets their own terms.
