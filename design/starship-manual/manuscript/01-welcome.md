# A ship the crew can understand

The relay keeper has offered the crew a job. Before they accept, someone checks the cutter's range, someone asks whether the hold can take the replacement board and someone looks at the service record for the port engine. Every answer belongs on the ship's record. This manual shows you how to get those answers and what to do when they change underway.

The ship is the crew's shared piece of equipment. Decide what jobs you want it to take and which systems it needs to do them. Its frame gives it a budget and physical limits. Each system has an installed rating and a current die. You can buy a good array and still find yourself working with worn sensors by the time you reach the relay.

## Start where you need to work

| If the crew needs to… | Turn to… |
|---|---|
| Choose a ship and pay for its systems | Chapter 2. Start with the frame, install the required systems and follow the Scout Cutter spending ledger. |
| Run a station or handle a fight | Chapter 3. Find the action, resolve its outcome and check any systems put under load. |
| Prepare and commit a jump | Chapter 4. Confirm the route, track charge and plotting, then hand off to the pilot. |
| Compare complete vessels | Chapter 5. Use the Scout Cutter, Free Trader and System Shuttle as starting builds. |
| Look up a procedure or copy a ship record | Chapter 6. The painted hull sheets follow the reference and operations records. |

## Before you leave port

Write down who is aboard, where they normally work and how much fuel, cargo and repair stock you actually have. Keep the ship record where everyone can see it. When a die steps down, tell the crew what that changes before they commit to their next action.

Use the *Player's Book* for learned abilities and personal injuries. Use the *Sector Architect's Book* to establish what is at stake on the route or when the crew arrives. Construction, travel and ship combat values in this manual are playtest proposals.
