# Encounters and opposition

Start with the encounter's purpose. The crew might need to reach a control room, protect a witness, identify a saboteur, negotiate safe departure or hold an exit. The opposition needs its own reason to act. Defeating everyone is one possible outcome, not the default requirement for every scene.

## Review the actual crew

Record the number and level of characters, current Grit, existing scars, treatment access, attacks, movement, control and supplies. A crew returning from a costly confrontation is not mechanically equivalent to the same crew starting rested. Safe breath can restore HP while Grit expenditure and lasting wound penalties remain.

Look for combined disabling effects. Several enemies repeatedly applying Dazed or Paralyzed can remove player decisions before HP damage appears severe. Start with ordinary opponents and add one unusual feature at a time. Every reinforcement that can join before safe recovery is part of the encounter's danger.

## Assemble an understandable roster

Choose a small number of actors you can run clearly. Give each a job: hold a route, pressure from range, disrupt a device, protect a person or retreat with information. The job grants no unprinted bonus or extra action. A profile with several actions chooses among them; it does not take every action each turn.

The seed profiles are authoring examples, not approved encounters. Before any table use, review their damage against the actual party's resistances, their access to cover, action count, healing cost and ability to focus attacks. A single remote turret behind inaccessible cover can be more dangerous than several ordinary crew in an open bay.

A security crew at the relay might want to stop a launch from leaving with a disputed board. Give them a place to stand, a reason to challenge the crew and an instruction about when to back off. A negotiator who can prove ownership has a way to end the encounter that uses the same facts as a pilot trying to get the shuttle away.

## Terrain and exits

Draw sight lines, blocked paths, cover and useful changes of elevation. Mark what a character can climb, open or disable and what requires an action. A locked route should have a stated access method; a map label is not a licence for an enemy to attack through a wall.

Write at least one plausible ending besides annihilation: surrender, withdrawal, a negotiated concession, evacuation, completing an objective or losing access while surviving. Opponents can decide to retreat without a new morale die unless a rule requires one. Their motives should explain that choice.

## Test and record before claiming balance

Use documented parties and supplies. Record rounds, actions denied, HP recovery, Grit spent, new wounds, retreats and which abilities dominated. Test open and obstructed terrain, different crew counts and varied healing access. An allocation checker or a clean PDF cannot substitute for these tests.

If a threat is revised after testing, record what changed and why. Keep an earlier result with its original profile version so later comparisons remain meaningful.

## Calculate a provisional Combat Value

Combat Value (CV) gives you a starting point for choosing a roster. All GoL values here are **untested authoring estimates**. First calculate a seed for each damaging profile. Then compare the roster to the test party and read the terrain, objectives and timing separately.

1. **Reference incoming attack.** A +1 explorer rolls 3d6 with no extra dice and deals 2, 3 or 5 Physical damage. The Weak, Standard and Strong probabilities are 35/216, 146/216 and 35/216. Reduce each outcome by the profile's Physical resistance, with a minimum of 1. Take the probability-weighted average.
2. **Endurance (E).** Divide printed HP by that expected incoming damage. This ratio approximates how many attacks the profile can withstand.
3. **Offense (O).** Use the first printed Main Action that deals one damage type across three outcomes. A reference explorer has a +1 defense and Physical resistance 1. Read the outcomes in reverse defense order, and apply resistance and the minimum damage per event before averaging. Assume one eligible target per round. The NPC's attack attribute is not added to the player's defense roll.
4. **Utility (U).** Start at 1.00. Use 1.10 for a documented situational advantage, or 1.25 for a pronounced complication such as a condition that can take away an action. Review additional advantages in the matchup. Mutually exclusive Maneuvers or Reactions do not each multiply the estimate. Track how often the option actually matters.
5. **Seed CV.** Multiply the square root of E × O by 3 and then by U. Round up once, minimum 1.

A Dock Watch officer has 12 HP and Physical resistance 1. The incoming average is about 2.16 HP, so E is about 5.55. Its 2/3/5 Physical attack gives O about 2.16 against the reference defender. With U = 1, its seed CV is **11**.

The comparison deliberately holds attack frequency and reference defenses constant. It omits cover, movement to contact, wound and Grit effects, healing, condition riders, attack access, and combinations of abilities. Review those factors before a session. When the primary action cannot be represented, leave the profile unrated. Field Medic has no damaging Main Action. Test its contribution in a mixed roster and record healing net of the cost and any actions it displaces. The rated subtotal of a mixed roster is incomplete.

## Choose the test budget

The initial level-1 standard reference is four Dock Watch officers, for **44 CV**. For four explorers of level L, the provisional test budget **B(L) = 44 + 8 × (L − 1)**. The eight-point slope is borrowed from HoL's testing hypothesis. Fixed HP and milestone ability choices can make the real growth curve uneven.

For a same-level party, multiply B(L) by party size divided by four. For mixed levels, add one quarter of B(level) for each explorer. Apply one comparison multiplier to the result. Keep the exact subtotal for comparisons.

| Comparison | Multiplier |
|---|---:|
| Easy | 0.75 |
| Standard | 1.00 |
| Hard | 1.50 |
| Extreme | 2.00 |

Three level-1 explorers have a standard comparison budget of 33. A crew at levels 1, 1, 3 and 3 has a standard budget of (44 + 44 + 60 + 60) / 4 = 52. Count every opponent who can join before safe recovery, and show when a wave enters. A blocked or optional opponent may contribute less than its paper value. Record that in the result rather than changing the printed seed between runs.

## Calibrate at the table

1. Start with the Berth Hold anchor and a documented level-1 crew. Record sheets, loadouts, current HP, Grit, lasting wounds and healing. Use the same crew when comparing a change in cover or starting range.
2. Repeat with three, four and five explorers and with and without healing access. Test both a fight and an objective-first approach. Record duration, damage, Grit spent, new wounds, actions lost and each side's objective outcome.
3. Add one source of pressure at a time. Breaker Room tests reach and access. Signal Cut tests conditions. Recovery Line tests healing and reinforcements. Record what each profile actually did and which hazard triggers fired.
4. Repeat at levels 3 and 7. Compare the observed level changes to the proposed budget slope. Revise the reference budgets separately from individual profiles. Adjust a profile's U only when repeatable behavior points to an advantage the baseline misses.
5. Record the catalog version, test conditions and sample count before reporting results. Compare the inherited design targets of roughly four rounds for a typical fight, six for hard and ten for extreme, and compare new wound rates with and without a healer. Treat these as investigation targets. Keep the provisional label until there is enough repeatable evidence to justify a change.
