# Encounters and opposition

Start with the encounter's purpose. The crew might need to reach a control room, protect a witness, identify a saboteur, negotiate safe departure or hold an exit. The opposition needs its own reason to act. Defeating everyone is one possible outcome, not the default requirement for every scene.

## Review the actual crew

Record the number and level of characters, current Grit, existing scars, treatment access, attacks, movement, control and supplies. A crew returning from a costly confrontation is not mechanically equivalent to the same crew starting rested. Safe breath can restore HP while Grit expenditure and lasting wound penalties remain.

Look for combined disabling effects. Several enemies repeatedly applying Dazed or Paralyzed can remove player decisions before HP damage appears severe. Start with ordinary opponents and add one unusual feature at a time. Every reinforcement that can join before safe recovery is part of the encounter's danger.

## No calibrated GOL encounter budget yet

HOL uses Combat Value, or CV, to organise encounter development, but its figures remain calibration seeds. The GOL profiles in Chapter 6 have no calibrated CV. Do not assign them HOL creature values or publish a guaranteed safe enemy count simply because their HP is similar.

The inherited design targets for a four-character reference party are roughly four rounds for a typical fight, six for a hard fight and ten for an extreme fight. Desired wound incidence is a design-test question, not something to force on a particular group. None of those durations is a promise for this kit.

For later calibration, write B(L) for a tested four-character standard budget. For N same-level characters, a candidate budget is B(L) × N/4 × difficulty. Proposed easy/standard/hard/extreme multipliers are 0.75/1/1.5/2. Mixed levels use the sum of each character's B(level)/4, then difficulty. There is no approved GOL B(L) in this edition. Do not substitute HOL's 32 + 8 × level seed as if it had been tested here.

## Assemble an understandable roster

Choose a small number of actors you can run clearly. Give each a job: hold a route, pressure from range, disrupt a device, protect a person or retreat with information. The job grants no unprinted bonus or extra action. A profile with several actions chooses among them; it does not take every action each turn.

The seed profiles are authoring examples, not approved encounters. Before any table use, review their damage against the actual party's resistances, their access to cover, action count, healing cost and ability to focus attacks. A single remote turret behind inaccessible cover can be more dangerous than several ordinary crew in an open bay.

## Terrain and exits

Draw sight lines, blocked paths, cover and useful changes of elevation. Mark what a character can climb, open or disable and what requires an action. A locked route should have a stated access method; a map label is not a licence for an enemy to attack through a wall.

Write at least one plausible ending besides annihilation: surrender, withdrawal, a negotiated concession, evacuation, completing an objective or losing access while surviving. Opponents can decide to retreat without a new morale die unless a rule requires one. Their motives should explain that choice.

## Test and record before claiming balance

Use documented parties and supplies. Record rounds, actions denied, HP recovery, Grit spent, new wounds, retreats and which abilities dominated. Test open and obstructed terrain, different crew counts and varied healing access. An allocation checker or a clean PDF cannot substitute for these tests.

If a threat is revised after testing, record what changed and why. Keep an earlier result with its original profile version so later comparisons remain meaningful.
