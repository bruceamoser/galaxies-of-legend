# Preparation and review tools

Choose a pressure that puts something the crew cares about in motion. Attach it to a location they know and two people who can take action. Use the worksheets to turn that outline into the notes you need at the table.

## Six situation frames

| d6 | Pressure | Two interested parties | A decision to prepare |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | A repair berth closes before a departure window | Port maintenance and a vessel carrying urgent freight | Spend time verifying a fault or accept a costly delay? |
| 2 | A charted route contains an unverified signal | Survey staff and a route operator | Share incomplete information, investigate or take another route? |
| 3 | A shipment's manifest conflicts with its seals | Cargo workers and the listed owner | Protect the cargo, the evidence or the schedule? |
| 4 | A service robot carries an unexpected instruction | Its work crew and a remote contractor | Isolate it, preserve its logs or complete the urgent work first? |
| 5 | Two communities claim the same salvage | Local residents and an outside recovery team | Who can establish access and what proof would either accept? |
| 6 | A station must ration a critical service | A cooperative and a well-supplied visiting group | Trade resources, change the schedule or find another source? |

These entries grant no technology, ancestry, item or encounter roster. Determine the actual access, route, hardware, witnesses and opposition before turning one into an adventure. Do not assume every frame requires combat.

## A scene review checklist

Can the players see what is at stake? Are obvious facts available without a gatekeeping roll? Do the listed actions have real ranges, costs and outcomes? Does opposition follow its own action budget? Are hazards triggered at a clear interval? Is there a way to leave? Which book owns each disputed rule?

Before any test, record the actual party, equipment, Grit and wounds. During it, note uncertainty and consequences without changing the foundation mid-roll. Afterward, separate a presentation problem from a numerical balance problem. A misread condition needs a clearer reference; an overpowered combination needs evidence about its actual results.

## Record a ruling

Choose the smallest ruling that fits the established rules and explain it to the table. Record the trigger, ruling, affected rule and observed result. A scene ruling applies to the situation at hand. A lasting change belongs in a system delta so everyone can see when it takes effect.
