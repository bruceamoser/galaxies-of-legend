# The Sector Architect

The Sector Architect describes a frontier worth exploring, gives its people understandable reasons to act and makes consequences visible. You do not need an answer for the whole galaxy. You need enough concrete information for the crew to choose its next move and see what changed because of that choice.

This is the base SA volume of the first three-book Galaxies of Legend Starter Kit. It provides a working preparation and adjudication framework, a small set of proposed threat profiles and reusable worksheets. It is not a finished setting encyclopedia, calibrated bestiary or starter mission.

## Use the right book

| Book | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Player's Book | All character construction, abilities and the complete Legend foundation, including damage, Grit, Dying, wounds, healing and conditions. |
| Starship Manual | Frame budgets, modules, equipment grades, station actions, Hull, ship combat, repairs, FTL and ship records. |
| Sector Architect's Book | Information, rulings, preparation, factions, opposition, hazards, rewards and campaign consequences. |

Do not shorten the Player's Book wound system into a different system during a session. Do not improvise a free ship module because an NPC's story mentions one. A new rule is a design proposal, not something the other books secretly omitted.

## Agree on the kind of frontier

Ask what the crew wants to do: survey, trade, negotiate, investigate, salvage or protect a community. Establish the tone, the kinds of personal stakes the players want and what they prefer to leave outside the game. Choose the scale of immediate travel and the status of the crew's ship together.

Record three useful facts: what keeps the crew together, what makes the next opportunity attractive and which obligation they cannot ignore indefinitely. These are hooks for choices, not instructions that force the players down one route.

## Prepare what can change

For the next session, prepare a place, a pressure, two interested parties, three useful facts and a way to leave. Know what happens if the crew does nothing. Leave enough uncertainty for their approach to matter. A map can be five connected locations with notes about doors and sight lines; it does not need a complete station blueprint.

Use the worksheets at the end to keep pending consequences, supplies and open rules questions visible. Distinguish a fact established in play from an idea that remains a possibility.

## Status of the base book

The Legend foundation is inherited from HOL; this volume follows the same bands, turn structure, resistance, condition and survival rules. New encounter guidance, threat values, hazard fixtures and reward examples here are **untested playtest proposals**. They do not establish safe encounter budgets or an expected number of wounds.

Ancestries remain placeholders awaiting Bruce's material. Do not invent their senses, resistances or environments from appearance. This package remains a coordinated reading review until the designer approves its rules and presentation for the next stage.
