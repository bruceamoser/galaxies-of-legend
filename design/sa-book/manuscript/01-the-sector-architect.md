# The Sector Architect

At Waycross, a relay keeper asks the crew for a lift before the next departure window. The port authority wants the relay left alone. Give each person a reason for asking, a way to help and a limit on what they can offer. The crew can take the job, negotiate different terms or walk away. Your job is to show what follows from their choice.

As Sector Architect, you describe the frontier, portray its people and adjudicate uncertain actions. Prepare the people and pressures that will be present when the crew arrives. Leave their decisions to the players. Use the rules when the outcome matters and the table needs a fair way to discover it.

## Start with the next session

Ask what kind of work the crew wants and what keeps them together. Decide where their ship is berthed, how far they can travel and which obligations are already waiting for them. Agree on the tone and any subject the group wants to leave outside the game.

Prepare a location, two interested parties, a pressure and several useful facts. Give the crew a way to leave. A map of five connected locations with a few marked doors can be enough. Chapter 3 helps you place those locations in a sector. Chapter 4 shows how to turn them into a situation.

## Keep the three books within reach

| Book | Reach for it when… |
|---|---|
| Player's Book | A character chooses an ability, takes a personal injury or needs the rules for recovery. Chapter 7 has the complete wound and treatment reference. |
| Starship Manual | The crew chooses a frame, works a station, takes a Hull hit or prepares a jump. |
| Sector Architect's Book | You need to decide what the crew can perceive, prepare a place, choose opposition or carry a consequence forward. |

The threat profiles and hazard examples here are playtest material. Chapter 5 explains how to review them against the actual crew before a session. The worksheets at the back give you a place to record the decisions you make and the results you observe.
