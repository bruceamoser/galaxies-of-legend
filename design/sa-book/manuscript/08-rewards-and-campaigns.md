# Rewards and continuing consequences

A reward can be pay, reliable information, access, an ally, a repaired obligation or an opportunity the crew could not previously accept. Make the terms clear enough that the players can decide whether the work is worth the risk. Money offered after a surprise cost is not the same bargain they accepted before departure.

## Keep character advancement intact

Use Player's Book Chapter 2. Levels 2-10 grant 8 plus the new level in DP, which may be saved; Adept and Master gates remain levels 3 and 7. Levels 3 and 7 each add one Grit without refunding earlier expenditure. HP does not rise automatically with level.

Agree on milestones in terms of meaningful accomplishments and campaign development. Do not add extra kill XP, bonus permanent HP, free discipline ranks or routine Grit refills as a reward system. An explicit new grant needs its own authored permission and review. A once-only ability remains once-only even if treasure would make a second purchase convenient.

## Quote a job in practical terms

Before acceptance, distinguish pay from expenses, access from ownership and a loan from a gift. Name who provides fuel, berths, replacement parts and medical supplies. When a price is not yet part of the catalog, record the local quote as a provisional campaign ruling rather than silently establishing a universal price.

The current market includes medical supplies at proposed 20/50/120-coin prices and the stated starting equipment. A ship's Ship Points (SP) do not convert into coins. This edition has no approved ship purchase schedule, construction labour cost or universal salvage percentage. Do not disguise those missing economic decisions as a completed trade system.

## Salvage and special equipment

Decide what survives the event and what the crew can reach, move and legally sell. A defeated drone does not automatically grant its attack as a learned ability or a fully portable ship module. Ordinary equipment can use the market's resale rule where a buyer exists; unusual hardware needs an explicit condition and quote.

Learning, owning equipment and acquiring a capability are different. Buying a med bay grants no treatment card; finding a targeting array does not give the crew space and power to install it. A special item must print the effect it adds. Appearance and rarity do not quietly increase damage or grant resistance.

## Let consequences follow established facts

Update who knows about the crew, what routes changed, which faction owes whom and which promise is now due. A faction's response should use its recorded resources and limits. Show some consequences soon enough that the players can recognise the connection to their choice.

A simple four-box faction clock can track a declared project: gathering support, gaining access, moving supplies and acting. This is an optional preparation tool, not a new action-resolution mechanic. Tick it only when an established event or meaningful elapsed time advances the project. Let the crew interrupt the actual work rather than merely attack a secret clock.

## Death, replacement characters and unresolved rules

Resolve the complete Dying and Last Words procedures before ending a scene. A campaign reward or a routine rest cannot reverse a final death. Any exceptional reversal must be explicitly authored; no such universal reversal is supplied by this base book.

A replacement character uses the Player's Book creation sequence and the campaign's agreed level. Current ancestry slots remain incomplete; do not fill a missing biological package with ad hoc benefits. Preserve the departed character's choices in the setting through relationships and consequences rather than automatically transferring every resource and obligation to the new sheet.

Keep a review log for unclear interactions and proposed changes. Record the trigger, ruling, affected rule and observed result. A clean separation between established foundation and open proposal makes the next design revision easier to trust.
