# Adversary catalog

Choose a profile for what it does in the scene. A guard can block a route while someone checks a manifest. A drone can force the crew to decide whether to reach its control point or take cover. The profiles here are playtest examples with no calibrated CV. Review the actual crew and location before combining them.

A profile is an NPC, not a completed player ancestry. Do not reverse-engineer a playable species from its HP. Equipment listed here supports the printed actions but is not automatically recoverable as intact treasure; decide what actually survives and state that honestly.

## Running a profile

Use the profile's HP, resistances, Speed, Stability and printed attributes. A player attacks with their own declared ability and one roll. When the profile attacks a player, that player makes the appropriate defence check and takes Strong/Standard/Weak effects on Weak/Standard/Strong defence. For NPC versus NPC, use the target's printed defence attribute and the same inversion. The profile's attack attribute is available when the rules call for the acting NPC's own check; do not roll a second opposed attack.

Every profile receives the ordinary action budget. It can move and choose one printed Main Action; it does not use every attack on its card. A robot or fixed weapon does not also get an extra operator turn. Conditions, cover, damage and minimum total damage follow the Player's Book. A nonliving profile specifies its repair boundary; being a machine does not supply unprinted blanket immunity to every condition.

## Defeat and surrender

Ordinary NPCs and devices are defeated at 0 HP. They do not receive player Grit, death marks, scar-weighted wounds or Last Words unless an explicit exceptional profile says so. A person may surrender before that point, and a machine can be powered down by an established control method. Use motive and access, not a secret second HP pool.

A profile's behaviour notes are tactics and motives. They do not cause free movement, forced fear, an extra attack or automatic access to a console. Give the crew observable signals of danger and a practical reason to choose a response.

## Choose a family

| Family | Scene pressure | What to show the crew |
|---|---|---|
| Crews | A rival wants the same cargo, berth or witness | Their objective, their route out and the terms they might accept |
| Security and synthetics | An automated or directed defense controls a route | Its boundary, sensor or warning cue and an accessible means of shutdown |
| Station fauna | Life in a habitat defends a nest or follows a food source | The signs of its presence and what draws it toward or away from the crew |
| Void entities | A local anomaly reacts to a particular signal or environment | The repeated sensory change and the conditions that sustain contact |
| Alien ecology | An organism protects territory, feeds or moves through its habitat | The terrain it uses and what lets the crew pass without provoking it |

| The Slop | A mobile colony feeds, engulfs prey or damages equipment | What it is moving toward, how its body attacks, and how to lure or contain it |

## Put a profile in a scene

At an impounded berth, a **Berth Extortionist** wants payment before releasing a tow cable. A **Credential Interceptor** treats the missing clearance as a separate problem. Decide who can authenticate the berth release, and show the crew the cable and badge scanner before they choose whether to negotiate, seek a witness or approach the controls. Neither profile gains authority from the other automatically.

At a recycling junction, a **Filter Eel** defends the flooded channel while a **Cryogenic leak** crosses the dry service route. Mark the grate and the coolant valve. The eel acts on its turn. The leak applies its own contact trigger from Chapter 7. Neither supplies an extra attack to the other.

At a dark relay, a **White Cinder** follows the emergency lamps. Put the light switch where the crew can reach it and let them observe which lamp attracts the entity before it attacks. The encounter can end when the crew redirects the light and clears the route.

## Run the extra options

Each profile prints its assigned shared abilities in full. Use the same Main Action, Maneuver, Reaction and movement budget as before. A guard that interposes a plate cannot also spend that Reaction on an opportunity attack. A drone that drops its cargo spends its Reaction even if the crew steps clear. Give players enough information to see the tradeoff, such as a marked lane, an open vent or a raised shield.

When adapting a profile, start with one option from the library at the end of this chapter. Check the trigger, the movement mode and the equipment. Add a different option only if it makes the adversary choose between two worthwhile responses. In a calibration test, record which option the adversary actually used and what changed. Avoid adding together the value of several abilities that all compete for the same action.

## When spacers say Slop

Slop is a spacer name for mobile colonies that coordinate as one body. Crews use it for puddings, jellies, molds and algae that move to feed or defend a meal. Show the creature before the crew has to commit. An explorer might see a boot suspended in a Clear Block or hear Bilge Walker’s pipes striking the deck. Each body has its own appetite and attack. Use its printed rules for engulfment, splitting or equipment damage.

A Cable Maw can eat an exposed branch circuit, and a Hull Grazer can reach a threatened exterior fitting. Name the actual circuit or fitting and the consequence before the creature gets there. These encounters use the personal stat blocks. For a resulting ship-system incident, use the Starship Manual. When the crew can see where the colony is heading, cutting power, moving a lure or sealing a route becomes a choice they can make before anyone rolls.
