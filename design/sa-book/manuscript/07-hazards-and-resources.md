# Hazards and resources

A hazard needs a visible sign, a specific trigger, an interval if it persists, a consequence and a way to reduce or escape it. Describe those facts before asking for a roll when the crew can perceive them. A hazard is not an excuse to make the whole group roll repeatedly until someone receives a wound.

## Declare exposure, not surprise arithmetic

Separate an immediate event from continuing exposure. A ruptured pipe can cause one impact, then leave a coolant hazard on the floor. Do not count the same contact as three damaging events unless three distinct triggers were established. Apply resistance and minimum personal damage to each actual damaging event; select a wound table only when the resulting harm triggers a wound.

The examples below are **untested SA authoring fixtures**, not a complete environmental simulator. Replace a value only through an explicit reviewed design change; a character's suit or trait grants only its printed protection.

| Hazard fixture | Visible trigger and interval | Proposed effect | Avoidance / response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open flame | Enter or remain in the burning area at the end of the turn | Apply Burning 1 once per exposed turn; resolve the condition at normal turn end, not as an extra separate fire hit | Leave the area; a plausible Main Action reduces Burning by 1 |
| Cryogenic leak | Contact the marked coolant flow | Apply temporary Freezing 1; a repeated contact is a new application | Shut the valve from safe access, avoid the flow or use an explicitly suitable protective item |
| Live conductor | Touch the visibly damaged powered cable | One 3 Lightning damage event per contact | Isolate power; use a plausible insulated method; a generic vac suit adds no unprinted immunity |
| Falling cargo | A declared unstable pallet topples into an occupied route | One 3 Physical damage event; a threatened character may use an appropriate defence roll to take 3/2/1 on Weak/Standard/Strong | Stabilise the load, clear the route or use cover; no free second dodge action |
| Decompression | An occupied compartment opens to vacuum | Immediate need for a working sealed suit or evacuation; the full continuing exposure numbers remain a separate unresolved hazard design | Close a reachable bulkhead, seal the breach or evacuate; do not invent a universal Fortitude difficulty 10 |
| Contaminated site | A marked radiation source or contaminated compartment | Use the Player's Book's explicitly proposed Radiation-to-Radiant mapping if adopted; determine dose timing and damage before commitment | Isolate the source, leave or use the exact protection printed on equipment |

Burning and Freezing apply their matching resistance and minimum damage; Bleeding bypasses resistance. A persistent source can reapply a condition after a character reduces it, but only at its stated trigger. Do not turn temporary conditions into starred ones because a location is dangerous.

## Time and supply records

Only count a supply when it is actually used by its rule: inventory after the use, fuel per charging segment, a medkit use per applicable care action, parts per ship repair. Record the final successful use before stepping d4 to out or offline. Do not charge one item through both an inventory die and a second invented charge count.

A safe place can permit a breath and HP recovery without restoring Grit or repairing wounds. One wound gets one full-hour treatment attempt per day across all healers. A ship med bay does not change that cap. Personal healing cost, medical supply use and ship structural repair are separate entries in the ledger.

## Travel and communication

Ordinary travel needs no routine roll or default exhaustion track. Call for a check at a consequential obstacle. Interstellar routes use Starship Manual Chapter 4; state distance, supplies and preparation limits before commitment. Radio access grants neither decryption nor unlimited sensing.
