# Hazards and resources

A hazard needs a visible sign, a specific trigger, an interval if it persists, a consequence and a way to reduce or escape it. Describe those facts before asking for a roll when the crew can perceive them. A hazard is not an excuse to make the whole group roll repeatedly until someone receives a wound.

## Declare exposure, not surprise arithmetic

Separate an immediate event from continuing exposure. A ruptured pipe can cause one impact, then leave a coolant hazard on the floor. Do not count the same contact as three damaging events unless three distinct triggers were established. Apply resistance and minimum personal damage to each actual damaging event; select a wound table only when the resulting harm triggers a wound.

The examples below are **untested SA authoring fixtures**, not a complete environmental simulator. Replace a value only through an explicit reviewed design change; a character's suit or trait grants only its printed protection.

| Hazard fixture | Visible trigger and interval | Proposed effect | Avoidance / response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open flame | Enter or remain in the burning area at the end of the turn | Apply Burning 1 once per exposed turn; resolve the condition at normal turn end, not as an extra separate fire hit | Leave the area; a plausible Main Action reduces Burning by 1 |
| Cryogenic leak | Contact the marked coolant flow | Apply temporary Freezing 1; a repeated contact is a new application | Shut the valve from safe access, avoid the flow or use an explicitly suitable protective item |
| Live conductor | Touch the visibly damaged powered cable | One 3 Lightning damage event per contact | Isolate power; use a plausible insulated method; a generic vac suit adds no unprinted immunity |
| Falling cargo | A declared unstable pallet topples into an occupied route | One 3 Physical damage event; a threatened character may use an appropriate defence roll to take 3/2/1 on Weak/Standard/Strong | Stabilise the load, clear the route or use cover; no free second dodge action |
| Decompression | An occupied compartment opens to vacuum | When the atmosphere is gone, apply the personal pressure and air procedures in Player’s Book Chapter 6 | Close a reachable bulkhead, seal the breach or evacuate; do not invent a universal Fortitude difficulty 10 |
| Contaminated site | A marked radiation source or contaminated compartment | 2 Radiant damage per ten minutes in the marked area as a proposed fixture. Apply normal resistance and harm | Isolate the source, leave or use the exact protection printed on equipment |

Burning and Freezing apply their matching resistance and minimum damage; Bleeding bypasses resistance. A persistent source can reapply a condition after a character reduces it, but only at its stated trigger. Do not turn temporary conditions into starred ones because a location is dangerous.

> **At the table.** Coolant is running from a broken pipe across the hatch threshold. Tell the crew that contact applies Freezing 1 and point out the accessible valve. If they shut it from the dry side, they can change the route before anyone is exposed.

## Time and supply records

Only count a supply when it is actually used by its rule: inventory after the use, fuel per charging segment, a medkit use per applicable care action, parts per ship repair. Record the final successful use before stepping d4 to out or offline. Do not charge one item through both an inventory die and a second invented charge count.

A safe place can permit a breath and HP recovery without restoring Grit or repairing wounds. One wound gets one full-hour treatment attempt per day across all healers. A ship med bay does not change that cap. Personal healing cost, medical supply use and ship structural repair are separate entries in the ledger.

Radio access leaves decryption and sensor coverage to their own rules.

## Pressure loss at a service lock

Show the pressure gauge dropping and tell the crew how much time remains. For a breach with warning, use a three-round clock and mark one segment at the end of each round. At three, the compartment is vacuum. An open lock that already vents directly to vacuum starts at three. Closing a rated bulkhead removes the sealed side from exposure. Patching the breach stops further loss, but a compartment that has already vented needs an air source before it is breathable.

Set an operable bulkhead control one move from the crew. A working control can close the bulkhead with an ordinary interaction. If it is damaged, state what can be accessed and the progress required before the attempt. Show the crew a second response, such as suit lockers or a route to a neighboring sealed space. The ship's reserve intervals apply only to sealed compartments with offline life support.

## Search a smoky berth

A cargo stack blocks a direct sight line, and light smoke imposes 1 Bane on sight-based checks down the service lane. A handheld sensor can register a moving heat source around the stack if its signal reaches the lane. On a successful scan give the measurable location and class the result supports. The sensor does not make a concealed target visible. A crew member who wants to identify the source can change angle, clear the smoke or seek a different sense.

If a Hidden explorer is already in the lane and a patrolling NPC might discover them, ask that explorer for one Agility roll. If an explorer is searching for a hidden NPC, ask for Reason. State what success can reveal, and use a Weak result to move the situation forward rather than restarting the same search indefinitely.

## Track a long crossing

For a ten-minute crossing of a radiator trench in extreme heat, announce the thermal clock and show the crew a cooled maintenance alcove. A short visit to the alcove pauses exposure. Ten safe minutes clears the clock. If three exposure segments fill before the crew reaches safety, apply Burning 1 once, then resolve it with the ordinary condition rules. A live steam outlet on the route is a separate contact hazard only if the crew actually enters its declared area.
