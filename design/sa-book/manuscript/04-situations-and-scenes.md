# Prepare situations and scenes

A useful situation has people who want incompatible things, a place where those wants meet and time for the crew to choose an approach. Prepare what the interested parties will do, rather than a required sequence of player actions.

## A one-page preparation procedure

1. State the central pressure in one sentence. Who needs something before a deadline?
2. Give two or three parties a concrete goal and a resource they can offer or withhold.
3. Draw three to five useful places, including a way in and a way out.
4. Place three leads that reveal the underlying problem.
5. Decide what ordinary success, partial success and withdrawal could change.
6. List opposition that might become active, including any reinforcements.
7. Record what happens if the crew waits or leaves.

The deadline is fiction first: a ship departs, a storm reaches the site, a shift ends. A clock can help track a declared pressure, but it does not replace an ability's printed timing or create additional turns for enemies.

## Define the immediate scene

Tell the players where they are, what they can perceive and what is happening now. Leave the next action to them. Ask for details only when they can change a decision. A new room does not require a perception roll to see its obvious door or a navigation roll to walk across it.

When a proposed method is incomplete, explain the missing requirement: power, access, tools, a willing witness or a physical route. That is more useful than asking for a harder roll that might appear to bypass the requirement on Strong.

> **Example · The closed relay.** The keeper needs a replacement board installed before the next scheduled transmission. Port control wants a signed safety clearance. The crew can interview the mechanic, inspect the old maintenance record or visit the relay and see the fault. If they wait, port control closes the window. Keep the relay, the board and the people in play even if the crew chooses to deal with them in a different order.

## Complications with concrete consequences

| Pressure | Example complication | Preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Time | The target can be reached, but a patrol is now closer | Earned access or progress |
| Attention | The correct record is found and the inquiry is logged | The information found |
| Resources | The repair works but consumes the named spare | The successful repair |
| Position | The route opens but leaves the operator exposed | The new route |
| Obligation | The contact helps and asks a clear favour | The help already given |
| Uncertainty | The scan locates the source but not its identity | The actual contact |

Do not charge an undefined future penalty or change which result band the dice achieved. A complication should give the players a fact they can respond to.

## Crossing scene scales

Use ordinary time while no immediate threat requires turns. Enter combat timing when actions and reactions must be ordered. Resolve Dying before ending turn timing; no scene transition grants a free safe breath to avoid a death roll.

Personal combat aboard a ship stays personal combat. A malfunctioning console is not an automatic Hull hit; a Hull hit is not automatically a wound to everybody inside. If one event creates both structural and personal consequences, declare both and use each scale's procedure.
