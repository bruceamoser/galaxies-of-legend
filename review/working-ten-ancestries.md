# Galaxies of Legend · The Working Ten

**Playtest discussion draft · 4 November 2026**

One human baseline and nine original lineages. The named places are example homeworlds or places of origin. A person can grow up elsewhere, adopt any culture and pursue any class.

**Try a character:** Choose one ancestry and two of its five traits. Each ancestry provisionally contributes **+2 HP, base Speed 6 and +0 starting coins**. The common values make it easier to compare the traits. Culture, background and class follow the normal creation rules. Homeworld is a story detail for this exercise, with no second package or language grant.

**Using traits:** Each selected trait grants one Boon on a check you make in its stated situation. Each trait can grant that Boon once per scene. Use at most one ancestry Boon on a check, then apply the usual cancellation and dice cap. Describe your approach and use the attribute it calls for. A trait supports a check the SA would already call for and uses that check's action and timing. The usual tools, access, protective equipment and environmental limits apply. Descriptions establish appearance and origin. The numbered traits list the mechanical benefits.

## 1. Human

**Origin seed:** Waycross, a junction where old transit rings connect stations built by different settlers. Some Humans have roots there going back generations. Others are the first in their family to see a station.

1. **Measure Twice.** When fitting yourself into unfamiliar protective gear, check whether straps or seals will shift under movement.
2. **Distance Rhythm.** Check to maintain a steady walking pace on foot while escorting a group over a long route.
3. **Load Relay.** Check to pass a bulky object between people in a narrow passage without dropping it.
4. **Read the Grip.** Check to anticipate which way a visible person will shift a load from their hand and shoulder position.
5. **Demonstrate by Hand.** Check to teach a willing person a simple manual procedure by demonstrating it with the actual equipment.

## 2. Tessari

**Origin seed:** Kett Anchor, a dense rocky world with steep settlements linked by gantries. Tessari have broad joints and segmented collarbones that spread force across the torso.

1. **Wide Stance.** Check to stay upright on a deck that abruptly tilts while you have both feet planted.
2. **Load Shoulder.** Check to hold a collapsing object briefly while another person withdraws from beneath it.
3. **Low Pull.** Check to drag a person free of an obstruction while your feet have solid purchase.
4. **Counterweight.** Check to control a hand-operated lever as its load swings or shifts.
5. **Solid Seat.** Check to remain in an unrestrained seat during a sudden jolt when you can brace against a solid surface.

## 3. Orivane

**Origin seed:** The Lattice Moons, a network of small stations built around rubble piles. Orivane tendons and balance organs let them respond quickly to changes in orientation.

1. **Spin Read.** Check to judge when a visible handhold on a rotating structure will come within reach.
2. **Soft Catch.** Check to catch an ordinary drifting object with a free hand while secured to a handhold or tether.
3. **Line Work.** Check to move along a fixed tether through an area where your feet lack purchase.
4. **Clean Handoff.** Check to transfer a casualty between two secured rescuers while all three lack firm footing.
5. **Centerline.** Check to align a carried object with a narrow opening while your footing or orientation shifts.

## 4. Nacreth

**Origin seed:** Siltwake, where tidal caverns run beneath dry settlements. Nacreth have ribbed throats and cheek folds that register changes in water pressure.

1. **Backwash.** While submerged, check to locate an active water intake from the flow against your exposed cheek folds.
2. **Crosscurrent.** Check to keep hold of a fixed line when a current pulls you sideways.
3. **Still Surface.** Check to cross shallow standing water without making an audible splash.
4. **Wet Seal.** Check to seat a compatible gasket by hand while the joint is slick with water.
5. **Bubble Trail.** Check to trace a visible stream of bubbles to its underwater source.

## 5. Silev

**Origin seed:** Knell Basin, a rock shelf crossed by long seams that hum during daily heating and cooling. Flexible mineral plates run along Silev forearms and ribs.

1. **Contact Pulse.** Check to locate the likely source of vibration within a machine by touching its exposed casing while it runs.
2. **Sound Joint.** Check to find a loose fastener by tapping an accessible joint and feeling the response.
3. **Wall Count.** Check to distinguish repeated impacts on the far side of a wall by placing a bare hand on it.
4. **Load Tremor.** Check to tell when a rigid support you are holding begins to shift under a changing load.
5. **Quiet Fit.** Check to guide two rigid components into contact by hand without an audible knock.

## 6. Ambril

**Origin seed:** Morrowline, a chain of shelters at the edge of a world's long night. Ambril heat-exchange folds lie close to vessels that supply the hands and feet.

1. **Cold Fingers.** Check to work a small mechanical latch while your hands are chilled and still able to move.
2. **Warm Contact.** Check to tell which of two safe-to-touch pipes is warming faster by comparing them with bare hands.
3. **Cooling Breath.** Check to maintain a steady pace while hauling gear through uncomfortable heat within your equipment's operating limits.
4. **Rime Step.** Check to cross a thinly frosted surface on foot without slipping.
5. **Steady Hold.** Check to keep a delicate tool steady while shivering in ordinary cold.

## 7. Pellori

**Origin seed:** Reedglass, a wetland world where Pellori tissues grow in a permanent mesh around a single nervous system. Their exposed sensory patches change texture around certain living cultures.

1. **Ferment Trace.** Check to locate the source of airborne fermentation odors in a breathable compartment.
2. **Root Touch.** Check to distinguish a living plant's recently damaged roots from older dead roots by handling them.
3. **Spore Smear.** Check to distinguish visible biological growth from inert residue in a sample safe to handle.
4. **Graft Seat.** Check to position a living graft against a compatible host before it is tied in place.
5. **Wet Bloom.** Check to find the point where damp is entering a habitat by following a visible gradient of biological growth.

## 8. Lethren

**Origin seed:** Gloam Arc, a moon with long eclipses and settlements built around shaded shafts. Lethren have broad pupils and finely patterned cheek membranes.

1. **Edge Light.** Check to pick out the outline of a stationary object in dim light that already permits sight.
2. **Glare Break.** Check to track a visible moving object as it passes behind intermittent glare.
3. **Silent Signal.** Check to convey one immediate instruction through an agreed facial signal to a willing observer with a clear view.
4. **Still Frame.** Check to spot a small change between two views of the same visible location.
5. **Sight Line.** Check to judge which nearby surface will hide you from a single visible observer as you move through dim light.

## 9. Inkleaf

**Origin seed:** Tern Orchard, a ring of suspended gardens. Inkleaf descend from canopy browsers uplifted generations ago. They have flexible wrists and broad gripping pads that spread pressure over fragile surfaces.

1. **Trellis Grip.** Check to climb an intact lattice with usable handholds without damaging it.
2. **Light Touch.** Check to lift a small fragile sample with bare hands without crushing it.
3. **Blind Reach.** Check to identify a familiar loose item by touch in a space you can reach but cannot see.
4. **Cable Walk.** Check to balance on a narrow fixed support while you have a separate handhold.
5. **Padded Catch.** Check to catch a falling fragile object within reach using two free hands.

## 10. Weldkin

**Origin seed:** Kiln Nine, an abandoned foundry now inhabited by descendants of organisms grown for heavy industrial work. Weldkin have living skin and woven ceramic tendons. They use the shared personal harm and healing rules.

1. **Tension Read.** Check to locate the point of greatest strain in a harness you are wearing under load.
2. **Torque Sense.** Check to stop turning a fastener by hand before its threads strip.
3. **Fine Pressure.** Check to apply a steady force to an ordinary hand clamp while another person works on the clamped object.
4. **Brace and Release.** Check to extract a trapped hand or foot by timing its removal to a machine's visible pause, when extraction is physically possible.
5. **Draw Straight.** Check to pull a jammed mechanical part free without twisting its housing when you can grip the part directly.

## What the research contributed

- **Starfinder** demonstrates a broad starting roster in which synthetic bodies and unfamiliar alien forms can sit beside Humans. For GoL, use that range of silhouettes and character pitches while writing each physiology, name and trait from scratch. [Paizo's Player Core overview](https://paizo.com/blog/starfinder-player-core-explained)
- **Traveller** makes aquatic and uplifted bodies playable, which raises practical questions about equipment and where a crew can operate. Nacreth and Inkleaf explore those questions with invented ecologies and bodies. [Mongoose Publishing's Aliens of Charted Space Vol. 3](https://www.mongoosepublishing.com/products/aliens-of-charted-space-vol-3)
- **Eclipse Phase** distinguishes a person's physical form from their memories and training. That suggests treating inherited and grown bodies as sources of specific physical affordances. Weldkin use this principle within GoL's existing character chassis. [Eclipse Phase Second Edition quick-start](https://eclipsephase.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/PS10001_EclipsePhaseSecondEdition_QuickStartRulesAcrimony_Nov2023.pdf)
- **Scum & Villainy** puts heritage and background side by side. I took that as a prompt to give each ancestry a clear first impression and leave upbringing and occupation to separate choices. [Evil Hat's official playbooks](https://evilhat.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/SaV-Release-Sheets.pdf)

## OpenBaD alignment and playtest questions

OpenBaD's parity review identifies the roster gap and flags traits that depended on missing environment procedures or discipline-named checks. Its completion plan leaves the species/homeworld split open under G6 and proposes eight homeworlds for that snapshot. This packet uses the requested ten discussion options, the current GoL checkout's ancestry terminology, and one shared origin budget. The OpenBaD conversation's later ruling uses situational triggers with one attribute chosen by approach. The common chassis and selectable traits follow the Legend character framework. Sources consulted: OpenBaD docs 02, 03, 10, 11, 13 and 17, the Legend chassis material and the matching review session in the OpenBaD database.

**Proposal and balance impact.** This packet would give the placeholder slot ten complete choices for trial use. The provisional Human values apply to all ten and the only new benefit is a bounded situational Boon from two selected traits. There are no changes to the engine, equipment, wounds or healing. Equal stats and an identical limit control some variables. Trigger frequency and the value of the best pair still need table evidence.

1. Ask everyone to circle three ancestries they'd want to play before reading the traits. Record which concepts they can describe back in one sentence.
2. Build the same character twice with different ancestries. Try both in a boarding rescue, a habitat investigation and a port scene. Keep culture, background, class and gear fixed.
3. For each selected trait, record how often it triggered and what it changed. Look for obvious pairings and low-frequency options, especially for Nacreth and Pellori in a ship-heavy campaign.
4. Compare overlapping concepts directly. Tessari should feel like holding or moving a load, Silev like locating mechanical evidence, and Weldkin like controlling force. If those distinctions disappear at the table, revise the triggers or combine concepts.
5. Ask which lineages people would keep, combine or replace, and whether a particular homeworld makes any of them easier to imagine.
